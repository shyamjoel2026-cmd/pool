## 1. What we are building

**The problem.** To buy a TV, AC or fridge, a family checks Amazon, Flipkart, Croma, Reliance Digital, the brand's website and a local dealer. They compare price, delivery, installation, warranty, card offers, returns and trust. Even after all that work they pay the price of one lone customer. Brands already give group prices to groups they can verify, such as company employees [1][2]. An ordinary household never gets that.

**What POOL does.**

1. **Paste any product link.** POOL identifies the exact product. It shows the honest best option — price with *your own* card offers, delivery date, installation and warranty.
2. **Press "POOL this".** Join an open pool of real buyers nearby who want the same kind of product, or start your own and choose when it closes. Authorised sellers then compete privately for the pool.
3. **Buy only if it's better.** If the pooled offer wins, you buy it through POOL's checkout, like Amazon: one payment, money safe until delivery, live tracking, one support desk. If Amazon is better for you, POOL says so.

**What we own, and what we don't.** We own no stock, warehouses or trucks. The authorised seller sells, invoices and delivers. The brand installs and gives the warranty. A licensed payment company holds the money. POOL runs the deal and protects the buyer.

| For | What they get |
| --- | --- |
| Buyer | A better all-in price, no pressure to buy, money safe until delivery, one desk for everything |
| Seller | Buyers who have already paid a booking. No ads, no haggling, no cash risk. Pays POOL only when a sale completes. |
| POOL | A fixed fee from the seller on every completed sale |

**Why us, and why now.** Plain price comparison is already free — 91mobiles, Buyhatke, and Google is testing Flipkart checkout inside Gemini [3]. So we don't compete on comparison. We compete on what those players don't have:

- the local authorised dealer's price, which is not online anywhere — you have to ask;
- the true installed price;
- each buyer's own card offers;
- the pooled deal itself.

Our starting edge: builder contacts (where households buy many appliances at once), dealer contacts (supply), and our own tech team.

## 2. One purchase, start to finish (example)

Ravi in Kondapur wants a 55-inch Samsung TV. *(All numbers are examples.)*

1. **He pastes the Amazon link.** POOL confirms the exact model. In the first months a person checks every match. Brands sell different model numbers on different sites [19], so we never guess.
2. **POOL shows his real options.** Amazon ₹30,000, tomorrow · Flipkart ₹29,600, 2 days · Croma ₹30,500, pickup today. None of his cards has a sale offer, so his best outside price is ₹29,600.
3. **He taps "POOL this" and pays a ₹2,000 booking**, fully refundable until dispatch. Now he is a committed buyer, not just a click.
4. **He joins an open pool.** POOL shows a pool for Samsung 55-inch TVs in his area. Another buyer started it and chose to close it on Friday at 8 pm. Ravi joins; he could also start his own pool and pick its closing time. Say 34 committed buyers by Friday.
5. **Verified dealers bid privately by Friday.** Each bid is a **guaranteed all-in price per buyer**, valid even if only one buyer completes. A dealer may also add a **Wave Drop** (section 5).
6. **Best offer:** Dealer B at **₹28,400 all-in**, wall-mount included, delivery Tuesday. That saves Ravi ₹1,200 against his best outside option.
   - His colleague Priya holds an SBI card. Say Amazon's 10% SBI sale offer [16] brings the TV to ₹28,250 for her. Then POOL tells Priya to buy on Amazon. We lose that sale and win her trust.
7. **He accepts.** He pays the balance now through POOL's checkout (UPI, card or card EMI), or at his door after checking the box.
8. **Delivery.** The dealer delivers. Ravi checks the box and gives his 6-digit delivery code. The brand installs.
9. **Payout.** The payment company releases the dealer's money minus POOL's fee. Small amounts wait until installation is confirmed and the wave closes.
10. **Wave close.** The wave ends with 34 completed purchases, so the shared refund pot holds ₹11,500. Ravi gets **₹338 back** to his card or UPI (section 5).
11. **After the sale.** His invoice and warranty card sit in his **Warranty Locker**.

## 3. How the money works — like Amazon and Flipkart

**The flow.**

- The buyer pays at POOL's checkout, run by a licensed payment company.
- The money sits in that company's escrow account, never in POOL's bank account. RBI's 2025 rules let the payout timing be set by agreement, so the dealer can be paid only after delivery [5][6].
- The dealer is paid after the delivery code, minus POOL's fee.
- Refunds go back to the buyer's original payment method.

**Ways to pay.**

- Full payment now.
- ₹2,000 booking now, and the balance at the door by UPI or card after checking the box.
- Card EMI.

**No cash.** The payment company's escrow cannot be used for cash-on-delivery [5]. Paying at the door after checking the box gives buyers the same comfort as cash-on-delivery, without cash.

**Our fee — the rule.**

- **The seller's price is the buyer's price.** If a seller quotes ₹28,400, the buyer pays ₹28,400. Nothing is added on top, ever.
- **POOL takes a fixed percentage from the seller's payout.** Starting rate card: **large appliances 5%, TVs 3%** (guess — check in the dealer test).
- **The rate is the same for every seller.** The buyer pays POOL nothing.
- **Why this and not a margin on top:**
  - The buyer sees the seller's own price, and the offer card states what POOL earns.
  - We don't earn more by pushing prices up or by favouring one seller.
  - It is the clean marketplace structure, so nothing needs restructuring when we raise foreign money.

**Where the money goes (example).**

| | TV ₹28,400 (3%) | AC ₹40,000 (5%) |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| Buyer pays | ₹28,400 | ₹40,000 |
| POOL fee | −₹852 | −₹2,000 |
| GST on POOL fee (dealer claims it back) | −₹153 | −₹360 |
| GST TCS 0.5% and income-tax TDS 0.1% (dealer's own tax credits) [12] | −₹148 | −₹209 |
| Held until installation is confirmed (10%) | −₹2,840 | −₹4,000 |
| Held until wave close (largest Wave Drop slab) | −₹1,000 | −₹1,000 |
| **Released to dealer on the delivery code** | **₹23,406** | **₹32,431** |
| **What the dealer keeps in the end, before any Wave Drop** | **₹27,548** | **₹38,000** |

**What we must set up before the first live order.**

- GST registration as an e-commerce operator.
- Monthly TCS filing, and TDS deduction [12].
- 18% GST on our fee.
- A grievance officer who acknowledges complaints within 48 hours and resolves them within a month [7].
- Seller details and return terms on every offer.
- A published rule for how offers are ranked.
- A written seller agreement covering refunds for defective, wrong or late goods [8].
- From 1 Jan 2027: any "was" price must be the lowest price of the previous 30 days [9].

## 4. How pooling actually gets a better price

There are four ways. We use all of them.

1. **Competition for a real prize.** Sellers bid privately for a whole pool of committed buyers, and one seller wins the pool, so the prize is worth bidding hard for.
   - Local dealer prices are not online, so this alone finds prices the internet cannot show.
   - Sealed bids let a dealer go below the brand's floor price (MOP) privately. India's competition regulator has penalised controlling dealer discounts [22], but brands still enforce MOP in practice [22], so dealers avoid cutting publicly.
2. **Real cost savings from batching.** One delivery day per area or tower, one installation team, zero ad spend and prepaid money all lower the dealer's cost per sale. Part of that saving goes to the buyer.
3. **Brand money.** Brands already give group prices to groups they can verify [1][2]. We turn households into a verified group and take each pool to the brands' bulk-sales desks.
4. **The Wave Drop.** Your settled-volume idea, fixed so it cannot be gamed (section 5).

**Honest limit.** During the Amazon and Flipkart festive sales, bank-paid offers (10% instant discounts, from 8–9 Oct 2026 [16]) often beat any dealer. A third of Flipkart's electronics sales are expected on EMI [17]. We don't fight that week. We win on installed price, service, baskets of appliances, and the rest of the year.

## 5. The Wave Drop — your idea, made cheat-proof

**Your rule stays.** The guaranteed price never goes up. The extra benefit comes only from **settled** purchases: paid, delivered, installed, replacement window over, no open dispute. Each pool is its own wave.

**The problem we found in the original tier design.** Take your example tiers (10 settled → ₹300 each, 25 → ₹700, 50 → ₹1,000) and a dealer who makes ₹1,800 on each unit *(example)*.

| Completed buyers | Dealer's profit |
| --- | ---: |
| 49 | ₹53,900 |
| 50 | ₹40,000 |

The 50th sale **costs** the dealer ₹13,900, because it lifts every buyer's bonus at once. So a smart dealer stops at 49 by delaying or saying "out of stock". The same thing happens at the 25th sale (−₹8,500) and the 10th (−₹1,200).

**The fix.**

- Every completed purchase drops its "slab" into a shared pot. The pot is split equally among all completed buyers in the wave.
- The dealer sets its slabs below its own margin. Then every extra sale **always** earns the dealer more — at least ₹800 in this example. Nobody stalls.
- Everyone gets the same share, so nobody gains by waiting to buy later.
- A fake account only counts if it actually buys and keeps the product. Then it is a real buyer.

**With the same slabs as your example:**

| Completed buyers | Pot | Each buyer gets back | Dealer's profit (₹1,800 margin, example) |
| ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| 10 | ₹300 | ₹30 | ₹17,700 |
| 25 | ₹5,200 | ₹208 | ₹39,800 |
| 34 | ₹11,500 | ₹338 | — |
| 49 | ₹22,000 | ₹449 | ₹66,200 |
| 50 | ₹23,000 | ₹460 | ₹67,000 (the 50th sale adds ₹800) |
| 100 | ₹73,000 | ₹730 | — |

The headline is smaller than "₹1,000", but it is real, and the dealer actually wants to reach it. A dealer with more margin can offer bigger slabs.

**How the pot stays safe.**

- The payment company holds back the largest slab (₹1,000 in the example) from every order's payout, so the pot is always fully funded. We never chase a dealer for money.
- On the wave-close date the pot is paid back to each buyer's card or UPI, and the unused holds go to the dealer.
- The dealer issues credit notes for the refunds. Our CA must confirm the GST treatment of a discount agreed before the sale.
- If a dealer cancels a confirmed order, it still pays that order's slab into the pot, from its security deposit. A dealer never gains by failing an order.
- The live pot shows only real completed purchases, with a real close date. Fake counters and fake countdowns are illegal dark patterns [10].

**Why it also grows POOL.** Every buyer now wants real neighbours and friends to buy, because that raises their own refund. It is a referral engine with no referral cash, and it only rewards real purchases.

## 6. Why nobody can cheat it

| Trick | Why it fails |
| --- | --- |
| 50 fake accounts or devices join | The guaranteed price is per buyer and doesn't depend on how many join. The Wave Drop counts only completed purchases. Dealers see only committed buyers (booking paid), checked against the payer's bank account or card and the delivery address, with no more than 2 units of one model per household. |
| 49 friends order, then 49 cancel | Cancelling before dispatch is free. That is our policy; the law allows cancellation charges only if we would pay the same when we cancel [7]. The pot counts only completed purchases. The one real buyer pays the guaranteed price and gets no bonus. |
| Return abuse after delivery | We don't offer change-of-mind returns on big appliances, and neither does Flipkart for TVs; defects go to service-centre replacement [4]. Defect claims pass the brand's check before a dead-on-arrival (DOA) replacement. |
| Dealer stalls near a tier | The Wave Drop design (section 5). |
| Dealer doesn't pay the bonus | The bonus money is held back from the dealer's payouts until the wave closes. |
| Dealer and buyer do the deal outside POOL | The buyer loses the POOL Promise and the Wave Drop. The dealer loses future pools. A time-limited clause in the seller agreement forbids it (lawyer to draft). |
| A reseller buys 20 units | Maximum 2 of one model per household. Bulk buyers go to a separate business lane. |
| Dealers collude on bids | Bids are sealed and never shown to anyone. Invites rotate, chains and brand desks bid too, and bid patterns are watched. Bid-rigging is illegal [22]. |
| Fake, grey or display stock | Serial-number photo at delivery, a GST invoice from the authorised dealer's GSTIN, and warranty registration with the brand. |
| A scam to steal the delivery code | The code lives only in the buyer's app. It is single-use and expires, and POOL never asks for it. |
| "Dealer changed its bank account" scam | Bank changes only after a call-back check and a waiting period. |
| A dealer uses fake buyers to farm rewards | No reward is paid before settlement. Ratings come only from completed purchases. A real case: one Meesho seller ran about 2,500 fake orders a month [20]. |
| Staff leak bids | Every view of a bid is logged. |

## 7. The POOL Promise — trust without owning trucks

Amazon, Swiggy and Uber earned trust nobody gave them on day one. They did it with guarantees, tracking, ratings and cashless payment. We do the same.

| Promise | What it means | How we fund and enforce it |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Price** | The price you accept never goes up | Guaranteed bids only |
| **Money** | Your money stays with a licensed payment company until you give the delivery code | Payment company's escrow [5] |
| **Date** | Delivered by the promised date, or cancel free plus a late credit paid by the dealer | Late goods must be refunded by law [8]; the late credit comes out of the dealer's held money |
| **Genuine** | Authorised dealer, GST invoice in your name, serial registered with the brand. If not genuine, full refund. | Dealer checks; security deposit |
| **Install** | Installed within the promised days. If not, we escalate to the brand and, if needed, book a brand-authorised installer paid from the dealer's held money. | 10% installation hold. Brands can limit the warranty when someone other than their authorised installer fits the product — Samsung does for ACs [21]. |
| **One desk** | WhatsApp desk and a ticket number for every complaint. We refund first and recover from the dealer. | Legal response times [7]. Consumer courts hold marketplaces jointly liable [11]. |

**Order status moves only on proof:** dealer confirms → dispatch photo → delivery code → serial-number photo and invoice → brand installation job number.

**Ratings:** only buyers who completed a purchase can rate. Dealers earn more pools by performing well. Nobody can pay for rank.

**How the Promise is funded:** the dealer's held money, a dealer security deposit (amount: guess — check), and a POOL trust reserve of 0.5% of sales (guess — check).

## 8. Where we start, and why

Swiggy and Uber each started in one city and made it work before going national. We start in **Hyderabad**, on two tracks that share the same dealers and the same platform.

**Track A — New communities at handover.** This is our unfair advantage: builder contacts.

- Hundreds of households buy ACs, fridges, washing machines and TVs in the months around handover, at one address (guess — check). The builder can give us access, and delivery days batch themselves.
- ANAROCK expects **63,700 Hyderabad homes to complete in 2026** [13]. It also counts about **63,000 delayed units** in Hyderabad, with developers talking of 9–12 month slips [14]. So we plan on **builder-confirmed handover dates**, not the completion dates registered with RERA.
- **Possession Radar:** builder handover schedules, plus manual lookups on the Telangana RERA portal (searchable by proposed completion date; it has a CAPTCHA and no bulk export; its content may be reused if credited [15]). No scraping.
- The goal: dealer bids are lined up *before* residents move in, so the community's price card is ready on handover day.

**Track B — Open Hyderabad pools through POOL Check.** This is the consumer habit you want to build.

- Anyone in Hyderabad pastes a link.
- Open pools for TVs, fridges and washing machines, by brand and size. Anyone can start one and choose when it closes (section 9).
- The big **Summer AC pool** opens in February 2027, before the AC season.

**Why not all of India on day one.** Our estimate: Hyderabad buys about 5,800 TVs a week, and even a bestselling exact model gets only about 90 buyers a week across the whole city. So pools fill by *brand + size + week in one city*, or *at one address*. The same TV often has different model numbers on different sites [19]. Group buying that ignored this failed before [18]. We go national when brands or national chains can deliver nationally.

**Products.** ACs, fridges, washing machines and TVs. Chimney, hob, water purifier and geyser only inside baskets. Not phones or laptops: online owns them and margins are thin.

**Timing.** We use the festive-sale weeks (from 8 Oct [16]) to *measure* prices, not to fight them. The first live pools start after Diwali, which is 8 Nov 2026 [25].

## 9. Pool time and delivery — the buyer chooses

**How long a pool stays open is chosen, not fixed.**

- Whoever starts a pool picks when it closes: 1 day, 3 days, a week, 2 weeks, or a date. These choices are only suggestions; any time can work.
- Before anyone starts a new pool, POOL shows the open pools for the same product in the same area, with their closing times. Joining an open pool keeps pools big, and big pools get sharper bids and a bigger Wave Drop.
- Every buyer also sets **"I need it by"**. Only offers that arrive in time are shown to them.
- Only the product sets limits, not us:
  - mutton for Sunday must close by Saturday evening;
  - sellers need a few hours to bid (minimum: guess — check).
- The countdown is always real. The buyer sets it, and it is never reset or faked [10].
- The trade-off is the buyer's to make:
  - a short pool means fewer buyers but still a guaranteed price;
  - a longer pool means more buyers, sharper bids and a bigger Wave Drop.
- Offers arrive when the pool closes. Each buyer accepts, or walks away and gets the booking back.

**Delivery: whatever other platforms offer, we offer.** For each category, the buyer gets the choices big platforms give:

- **Home delivery:** standard, express where the seller can do it, or a scheduled slot for big items.
- **Store pickup with a code**, like the Croma "pickup today" option.
- **Installation and demo booking** for appliances, done by the brand.
- **Open-box check at the door** before giving the code. Flipkart does open-box delivery too [26].
- **Pay on delivery** by UPI or card at the door.
- **Exchange of an old product** through the seller (test).
- **Card EMI.** No-cost EMI only when the seller or brand pays for it.
- **Returns and replacement** follow the same category rules as big platforms. For example, TVs get service-centre replacement [4].
- **Who delivers:** the seller's own staff, or a partner POOL books. Porter, Shadowfax and Delhivery serve these needs [27][28][29]; rates still to confirm. Delivery cost is either inside the seller's price or shown as a fee before payment.

**One honest gap: cash on delivery.** Our payment company's escrow cannot take cash-on-delivery money [5]. We start with UPI or card at the door. Cash comes later, only through delivery partners that collect and send cash, once our lawyer and CA approve how our fee gets collected on those orders.

## 10. POOL for every sector

**Founder decision:** POOL covers every sector, one at a time.

**One engine, switched on sector by sector.** The steps never change:

1. Buyers commit.
2. Sellers bid privately.
3. The price is guaranteed.
4. The buyer pays through POOL.
5. The buyer collects with a code, or gets it delivered.
6. The seller is paid, and the Wave Drop refund goes out.

What changes per sector:

- how long pools can run (section 9);
- delivery style (section 9);
- what we check about the seller — a food-safety licence for a meat shop, GST and brand authorisation for a dealer;
- how much staff time an order can afford — small orders must run almost without staff.

**How we pick the next sector.** Six questions:

1. Do buyers commit in advance?
2. Are they in one place, or served by one seller?
3. Does the seller's cost really drop when demand is known ahead?
4. Is it the same product for everyone?
5. Can the buyer check it at handover?
6. How often do people buy it?

| | Sunday mutton | College laptops | Appliances at handover | Daily groceries |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Commit in advance? | Yes | Yes | Yes | Hard — 10-minute apps win |
| One place or one seller? | Area-wise | One campus | One community | — |
| Seller's cost drops? | Yes: no waste, planned buying | Some | Some: batched delivery | Margins too thin |
| How often? | Weekly | Every new batch | Once in years | Daily |
| Our read (to test) | **Best fast test** | Good seasonal | **Pays the bills** | Avoid for now |

Grocery group-buying has already struggled in India [18][30]. Phone retailers report very thin margins — about 1% net [31]. We leave both for later.

**Proposed order (founder to confirm).**

1. Appliances continue.
2. A cheap **Sunday mutton test**: two Sundays, 2–3 areas.
3. A **college laptop** pilot at one college.
4. After that, one sector at a time, each passing the six questions.

**Sunday mutton — how it runs.**

- **Any day up to Saturday evening:** buyers start or join their area's Sunday pool. They pick the shop, the cut and the kilos, and pay.
- **Shops bid** for the area's Sunday batch, or a chain gives a pool price.
- **Saturday night:** the price is fixed and every buyer gets a code.
- **Sunday:** pickup in a time slot, or delivery. The shop weighs and hands over, and gets paid after the code.

Honest points:

- **Where the saving can come from.** ₹700 for ₹1,000 mutton is unlikely to come from the shop's margin alone. It has to come from a shorter supply chain, such as batch buying through licensed butchers, and from having no unsold stock. It is a target to test.
- **Comparison.** Online meat brands such as Licious and FreshToHome, and quick-commerce apps, are shown side by side with the pool price.
- **Money.** A ₹1,350 order earns us about ₹65–70 at 5% (guess). Mutton's job is to build a weekly habit in each area, not to pay the bills.
- **Sneha.** Sneha is best known for chicken; whether its outlets sell mutton is still to check. Chicken pools work either way.

**College laptops.**

- A college is a verified group (student ID), and each year brings a new batch wanting the same few models.
- We pool per college and take the demand to brands' bulk desks and distributors.
- Brands already run student offers, so our pooled deal must beat them.
- Best timing: admissions (July–August) and before placements.

**Levers that improve the numbers:** fewer minutes per unit (batching, tools), bigger baskets, more UPI and fewer card payments.

## 13. How this gets big — step by step, each step earned

1. **Hyderabad works.** Target: 300 completed units in one month by March 2027 (our bar).
2. **The biggest cities.** Same playbook. ANAROCK expects 5.4 lakh homes to complete across the top 7 cities in 2026 [13].
3. **Brands at the table.** Wave pricing from brand desks becomes normal.
4. **Everything big for the home, pooled.** Interiors for identical flats, furniture, rooftop solar, EV chargers, AC servicing waves. Each one is tested before we commit.

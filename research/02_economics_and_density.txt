HEADLINE: The original plan (paste an Amazon link, pool the exact TV model across Hyderabad or India, let dealers bid, add a margin) fails on two numbers. First, density. Hyderabad buys roughly 4,600 to 6,900 TVs a week. Even a bestselling model gets fewer than about 90 buyers a week. To fill a 50-unit pool in 7 days while capturing 2% of in-market buyers, the pool would have to cover about 43% of the city's entire TV demand. Second, headroom. A general-trade dealer earns about 8-15% on a TV. Electronics Mart, Hyderabad's biggest chain, makes only 14.4% gross and 6.1% EBITDA across all categories. Festive online prices also carry bank discounts (10%, capped), exchange offers and no-cost EMI, all paid for by brands, banks or the platform, which no dealer can match from its own margin. So a Rs 30k TV cannot give the buyer a clear saving and also pay the platform a 5-7.5% cut, especially in October. Speed makes it worse: Flipkart and Amazon deliver and install TVs the next day in big cities, and quick-commerce players are pushing appliances to same-day or 30 minutes. The idea only works where density is physical rather than digital. That means new gated communities at possession, where one 400-home tower cluster can produce about 20 appliance units a week in one place. It also means selling a basket led by ACs, fridges and washing machines (better margins, installation matters, local service) rather than a single online-heavy TV SKU. The founder's builder and dealer network is the real edge. The AI link scanner is not a moat and should wait until dealers have shown headroom on paper.

RISKS: Headroom risk (the biggest): dealer all-in delivered and installed prices may come in at or above the best festive online price for a card-holder. If so, nothing is listable and the model has no margin. TVs are the most exposed; ACs the least. | Density illusion: registered demand is spread across many brands and models, so few brand lots reach 15-20 units and the platform becomes a slow one-unit quote service. | Speed gap keeps widening: same- or next-day appliance delivery (Flipkart top 66 cities, quick commerce testing 30-minute appliances) makes a 7-14 day pool wait harder to justify each year. | Brand retaliation: brands enforcing MOP may penalise dealers who post below-MOP pool prices, and online-only models cannot be bid on offline at all. | Weather and seasonality: AC demand swung from a record 12.5-13M units in FY25 to a forecast 11-11.5M in FY26 (ICRA); one mild summer can halve a season's pool volume. | Housing cycle: Hyderabad sales fell 23% in 2025. Fewer launches today means fewer possessions in 2028-29, and the possession wedge is finite per city (about 45-60k households a year, estimated). | Service accountability: in a pool, the buyer's warranty, returns and dead-on-arrival claims sit with the dealer and brand. One bad dealer can destroy trust in a whole community through its WhatsApp group. | Distribution copycat: MyGate and other community apps, which already sit inside tens of thousands of societies, could copy the model once it is proven. | Unit-economics fragility: at about Rs 1,500 contribution per unit, the team needs about 780 units a month to cover Rs 10 lakh monthly costs (blueprint's own arithmetic). That is dozens of communities in possession at the same time.
RECS: Drop the 'paste link, India/city-wide exact-model pool' as the core product. Keep link-paste only as the entry screen: a free price check that routes people with no suitable pool to the cheapest option via affiliate links (Flipkart 3% on large appliances). | Pool at brand + category level over 14-30 day waves, inside physical clusters (one community or one 10 km zone), never at exact model city-wide. Let buyers pick 'this brand, any model with my specs' or 'best value for my specs' to concentrate volume. | Lead with ACs + fridge + washing machine baskets and treat TVs as an add-on. Set a lower commission for TVs (about 2-3%) than for ACs (about 5-7.5%), or list TVs only where dealer quotes clear the pricing rule. | Make prices fixed per unit from sealed dealer bids, count only paid orders toward a lot, and never promise earlier buyers a price that later volume unlocks. This neutralises both abuse cases in Q3 at no extra tech cost. | Experiment 1 - dealer headroom test (about Rs 15k, 2 weeks, run across 8 Oct - 8 Nov). Get written, non-binding all-in quotes (GST, delivery, installation) from 12 Hyderabad dealers (multi-brand, brand-exclusive and Electronics Mart-type chains) for 6 products: 43-inch and 55-inch LG and Samsung TVs, a 1.5-ton 5-star inverter AC, a 250-340L fridge and a front-load washing machine. Ask for 4 volume scenarios: 1 unit, 20 of the same model, 20 of the same brand in mixed models, and 20 mixed brands. The same day, screenshot the best online effective price (after a typical bank offer, before exchange). Kill or pivot if fewer than 3 of the 6 products show at least a 7% gap at the 20-unit level. | Experiment 2 - possession-desk trial in 2 communities using the founder's builder contacts (about Rs 40-50k: standee, need forms, 2-3 weekend desks, a WhatsApp group). POOL handles no money; the winning dealer invoices and collects directly. Pass if at least 25% of handover households register, at least 2 units per household are recorded, at least 30% are flexible on brand or model, and at least 10% of registrants buy at the quoted community price within 30 days. | Experiment 3 - link-paste concierge smoke test (about Rs 25-30k: a one-page form plus Rs 20k of Hyderabad-targeted ads and posts in local deal groups): 'Paste any Amazon/Flipkart link, get a verified Hyderabad authorised-dealer price in 24 hours'. Humans get the quotes; no AI or scanning is built. Measure cost per submitted link (target under Rs 150), share of links that are online-only models, share where a dealer beats the best online price by at least 3% (target 30% or more), and conversion (target 5% or more). If this fails but Experiment 2 passes, the consumer app is out and the community model is confirmed. | Do not spend on the AI spec extractor or multi-platform scanner until Experiments 1 and 2 pass. A spreadsheet plus manual price checks covers the first 3 communities. | Keep business buyers (hotels, schools, offices buying 20-100 units) as an opportunistic side line through the founder's dealer network. Skip corporate employee programmes, because Samsung and LG already give 7-30% directly.
